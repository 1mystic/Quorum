§ 01 / spine

Six streams. Every statistic written once.

A society's complaint, a club's task and an NGO's volunteer request are one shape wearing three names. Quorum maps every vertical onto six canonical streams, so survival analysis is implemented once rather than once per community type.

§ 02 / reading

Most of this product is uncertainty, so most of the design is too.

A survival curve is mostly its band. A control chart is mostly its limits. A conformal ETA is an interval — there is no point estimate at all. And for a community in its first months, the most common tile on the screen says not enough data yet.

Uncertainty you can read off a scale.

In this direction a confidence band is bounded by hairlines and filled with a fine rule, never a soft wash. Every interval terminates in real tick caps against a real graticule. You can put a straightedge on it.

Control limits are drawn in a different language from confidence bands, because they are a different object: a decision boundary, not an estimate. Confusing the two is how a committee ends up 95%-confident about a threshold.

§ 04 / method

From your record to a number you can defend.

01 MAP

Streams, not schemas

A vertical manifest maps your entities onto the six streams. Nothing is rewritten per community type.

02 GATE

Minimum n, declared up front

Each method states its floor — 30 events for a survival curve, 20 periods for control limits, 5 members for any published cell.

03 WRAP

Every figure in an envelope

Value, n, interval, interval kind, method id, and the assumption checks the service ran on its own output.

04 NARRATE

The model reads, never computes

The assistant's tools return envelopes. It may quote and explain; it may not do arithmetic. Enforced by a grounding test.

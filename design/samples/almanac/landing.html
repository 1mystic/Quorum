The data spine

Six streams, and every statistic is written once.

A housing society, a robotics club and an alumni chapter look nothing alike. Underneath, they are the same six observable processes — so survival analysis gets written once, not once per community type.

01member_lifecycleJoin, activate, lapse, exit. Feeds retention curves and lapse risk — who is quietly on the way out, with how much certainty.
02request_flowAnything that opens, gets assigned and closes. Complaints, maintenance, task boards. Feeds Kaplan–Meier, control charts, queueing and the ETA above.
03ledgerSigned money movements with a counterparty. Maintenance dues, contributions, expenses. Feeds forecasting, runway and Benford screening.
04participationAttendance, RSVPs, volunteer hours, votes cast. Feeds engagement segmentation and the fairness reports.
05signalFree text and ordinal ratings — complaint bodies, survey answers. Feeds topic mining, near-duplicate detection and ordinal models.
06decisionPolls, ballots, budget allocations. Feeds social choice — including the disclosure that your poll had a Condorcet cycle and the "winner" depended on the counting rule.
Insight packs

Four packs. Each one answers a question a committee actually asks.

Pack 01 · Operations

Is this getting better or worse?

Kaplan–Meier resolution curves that count the still-open requests instead of quietly dropping them, EWMA control charts that separate a real shift from a noisy week, and Erlang-C staffing for the guard roster.

Pack 03 · Foresight

What is coming, and how sure are we?

Dues collection forecasts with prediction intervals, calibration scoring that tells you whether last quarter's forecast was actually any good, and conformal ETAs with guaranteed marginal coverage.

Pack 04 · Voice

What does the community actually want?

Social choice over polls, participatory budgeting with a fairness report, ordinal survey models, and k-anonymity floors so a per-wing statistic over three households is never published.

Pack 02 · Comparison — ships last

Is the new vendor actually better?

Empirical-Bayes shrinkage so a vendor who is 3-for-3 does not outrank one who is 47-for-52, plus proper experiment design. Deliberately last: it needs accumulated history before it can say anything honest.

How it works

Four steps, and the third one is the whole product.

Map your community onto the streams

A vertical manifest does it for you — a housing society's complaints become request_flow, its maintenance bills become ledger. A club's task board becomes the same request_flow. Nothing is rewritten per vertical.

Switch on the packs you need

Ten flats do not need a bandit allocator. Each tenant enables the packs that match its size and its questions, and every pack states its minimum sample before it will speak.

Every figure arrives inside an evidence envelope

A statistic cannot cross into the interface as a bare float. It carries n, its interval and the interval's kind, its method id, and the assumption checks the service ran on its own output. If a check fails in a way that blocks interpretation, the value is suppressed and the reason takes its place.

The assistant narrates — and only narrates

The language model's tools return envelopes. It may quote a value, its interval and its n, and it may explain a failing check. It may not do arithmetic on two envelopes or state a figure that is not in one. That is enforced by a grounding test, not by a polite instruction in a prompt.

11,204still-open requests that most dashboards silently drop

An average resolution time computed from closed tickets only is biased downward, because the slow ones are exactly the ones still open. Quorum censors them properly and reports the count on the face of the figure. Any service that averages only closed items is a defect here — there is a permanent regression test for it.

Bring your community's own record.

Housing societies, campus clubs, NGOs, alumni chapters, co-ops, sports clubs, guilds. Onboard by slug, map your streams, and watch the tiles honestly say not enough data yet until they don't.
